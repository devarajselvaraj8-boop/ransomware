3. SCIENTIFIC DISCUSSION: WHY ACCURACY ALONE IS INSUFFICIENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In malware family discrimination, high accuracy can often mask dangerous blind spots:
  (1) Severe Class Imbalance: Class 'L' represents only 9.31% (33 test samples).
      A naive classifier predicting only 'G' and 'E' would achieve >90% overall accuracy
      while completely failing to detect family 'L'. Macro F1 and Balanced Accuracy
      are essential to penalize minority misclassification.
  (2) False Negatives vs. False Positives: In defensive cybersecurity, a False Negative
      (allowing ransomware to run undetected) results in irreversible data encryption,
      whereas a False Positive (flagging benign activity) causes operational friction.
      Evaluating per-class recall is therefore paramount.
  (3) Perfect Baseline Score Context: Both Random Forest and XGBoost achieve 1.00
      accuracy across all partitions. This indicates that the 46 post-execution sandbox
      summary features provide trivially separable signatures among the 3 families.
      However, as demonstrated in our leakage analysis, this does NOT demonstrate early-stage
      detection capability during ongoing execution.
